Recognized with an Honorable Mention at the DiscoverArch Student Project Awards 2025, this project reimagines public sanitation as vital, visible civic infrastructure. Historically relegated to poorly lit edges of sites, public toilets often evoke hesitation and reinforce perceptions of risk. As Taipei’s urban sprawl increases transit dependence, safe, accessible pause points have become an essential daily requirement.

Located at the Linkou Bus Transit Station, this pavilion transforms a roundabout island into an urban landmark. It elevates the public restroom from a hidden utility to an active, shared space by merging sanitation with energy production, connectivity, and safety.

The design introduces a hexagonal, one-way circulation system with dedicated entry and exit doors. This deliberate flow ensures privacy, prevents face-to-face encounters, and supports gender-inclusive access. Prefabricated, multifunctional columns serve as structural supports, partitions, and integrated service shafts for plumbing and electrical systems.

Beyond basic utility, the pavilion operates as a generative node. An on-site underground biogas system processes waste from vacuum toilets to power lighting, ventilation, and integrated Wi-Fi and device charging stations. By transforming waste into energy and replacing hidden vulnerability with visible, modular design, the project proves that ordinary infrastructure can deliver dignity, safety, and collective urban value.
