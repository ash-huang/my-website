At George Leland Elementary, the landscape is reengineered from a passive surface into an operative infrastructure. This project establishes a new spatial datum, utilizing calculated topographical shifts to organize recreation, environmental mitigation, and outdoor learning. An amphitheater, carved into the building's incurved corner, creates a concave gathering zone that anchors the site’s academic programming.

Beyond this central core, the playground is restructured as a sequence of offset zones. Rubberized mounds and wood-chip challenge courses introduce a three-dimensional terrain that dictates movement, while strategic vegetation and pathways act as structural buffers between age-specific areas.

Simultaneously, the ground plane functions as a working ecological mechanism. A network of bioswales and rain gardens, planted with river birch and native grasses, frames the vehicular transit zones. This system actively captures and infiltrates stormwater runoff, integrating civic hydrology directly into the daily student experience. By treating both recreation and ecological management with equal structural rigor, the project establishes a resilient, dynamic foundation for the campus.

Located on the incurved corner of the L-shaped school building, a concaved space surrounded by plantings, with two rows of seating, provides a quiet, amphitheatre like outdoor environment for multifunctional purposes such as outdoor classes and school performances.
Playground consists of a wood-chip surface challenge course play area, an organic landscape that enhances natural play and exploration; rubber surfaced mounds that give a three-dimensional aspect experience; swings and a basketball court that were most required by the students, staff and parents. Paths and vegetation separate but also provide connection to different play areas, providing as a buffer to allow children to access play spaces safely.
