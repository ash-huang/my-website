This studio redefines interactive experiential space by integrating narrative-driven design with physical interaction to simulate the 'experience of nature.' The methodology progresses from narrative scripting, AI-assisted visualization, and architectural sectioning to the physical fabrication of a functional installation. Conceptually, the project intertwines Carl Jung's individuation process with the hydrological cycle across a five-act, six-scene spatial sequence. Each scene translates psycho-spiritual and ecological transitions—from pristine glacier to drought, evaporation, and vertical hydroponic rebirth—into atmospheric sensory factors (light, temperature, humidity).

The initial scene, an underground cistern, is manifested as a physical interactive prototype. Parametrically generated in Grasshopper and 3D-printed as a monolithic, double-skin PETG funnel, the device encapsulates water, a rotating light-distorting core, and rising air bubbles that mimic melting glacial ice. Driven by Arduino and Unreal Engine, the system maps human proximity to breathing light frequencies, establishing a real-time feedback loop between physical materiality and digital simulation.
