"Mutualism" explores an intertwined architectural concept, uniting a fashion workshop and a photography studio within twin live-work residences. Designed for a future Hsinchu City driven by bicycle mobility, the architecture seamlessly integrates elevated ramps and indoor bike parking to blend movement with daily life.

Visually, the twin structures interlock through a 1:3 volumetric proportion. Dual entryways separate commercial spaces on lower levels from private residences above, connected by layered mezzanines for a gradual work-life transition. Staggered split-levels create dynamic spaces in the south building, inducing visual access to different layers. While appearing independent above ground, the buildings join at the basement level through a shared photo studio—striking a balance between autonomy and synergy among architecture, industry, and city.
